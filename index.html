04 / A second pair of eyes

The coach

Aperi never touches your photograph. It doesn't generate images, retouch them, or edit anything. It reads the frame you're composing and the settings you're using, and tells you what it sees, the way an instructor looking over your shoulder would. The shot stays entirely yours.

Why not just ask ChatGPT?

You can, after the fact: export the photo, upload it, type in what you shot it at. Aperi is on the camera while you shoot. It reads shutter, aperture, ISO and focus mode straight from the body, so it never guesses the exposure from pixels. It answers in seconds about the frame in front of you, not the one from last week. And with earbuds in or the watch on, you never take your eye off the scene. A chatbot is a fair critic of a finished picture. This is a coach for the next one.

  • It knows your settings

    Not guessing from pixels. It reads shutter, aperture, ISO and focus mode straight from the camera.

  • It knows your bag

    Tell it which lenses you brought and it can suggest switching to one you actually have with you.

  • It speaks

    Ask out loud, hear the answer, keep your eye where it belongs.

Two real sessions

Both of these happened. The coaching quoted is what the app actually replied to the first frame in each pair, written before the second photograph existed and checkable against the EXIF.

Shifen Waterfall: moving water

The coach

The main issue is your shutter speed of 1/50. It is in a no-mans-land for moving water, making the falls look messy rather than intentional.

To get that classic, silky water, switch to Manual mode, keep ISO 100, and close down to f/16 to force a longer shutter speed, aiming for 1/2 or slower. You will need a tripod.

You either freeze water completely at 1/1000 or paint with it at 1 second. Let us slow it down.

The advice named 1/2 of a second; the next frame was shot at exactly that. Both on the same 18mm prime and ISO 100, so three stops of aperture bought the slower shutter. The framing changed too: APS-C crop mode was switched on and the tripod moved a step closer, so the water is not the only difference you are looking at.

Night street, Taiwan: where the colour lives

The coach

Your brightest highlights, like those blue and white neon signs, are completely blown out.

In night street photography, colour lives in the highlights. Because you are in A mode at exposure compensation 0, the camera is trying to make the dark asphalt neutral, which overexposes the signs.

Dial your exposure compensation down to -1.3 EV. This will protect those rich neon colours and clean up your shadows.

Let the darkness frame your light.

The advice asked for about a stop and a third less exposure; the frames measure 0.99 EV darker. The camera also moved to Manual, which stopped auto-ISO wandering between 800 and 3200 from one frame to the next, and stopping down to f/2.8 put more of the street in focus. It is meant to be darker. That is where the neon gets its colour back.

07 / Questions

Questions people ask

The short answers. The long ones are in the sections above.

Does it work with my Sony camera?

The A7 V is tested on real hardware before every release, over USB-C and Wi-Fi. The A7 IV, A7R V, A7C II, A1 II, A9 III, A6700 and ZV-E1 share the same protocol generation and should work, but we have not had one in hand. If you own one of those, you are exactly the tester we want.

How fast is the coaching?

Measured, not estimated: 0.9–1.5 seconds from a wrist press to the picture on the watch; 2.5 seconds on the best run from an earbud double-press to the first spoken word, 3.3–4.2 seconds typically; 4.2 seconds from a tap in the app over USB-C. Your network and lens move those numbers.

Do I need an API key?

No. The coach works the moment you open the app on Aperi's own key. If you would rather use your own key from Google, Anthropic or OpenAI, you can, and it stays on your phone.

Is it really free?

Every camera feature is free forever: live view, tap to focus, the remote shutter, exposure, the histogram and grids, the card browser. During the beta the coach is free too: 10 replies every 24 hours on our key, or unlimited on your own key.

What happens after the beta?

Camera control stays free. The coach becomes a subscription, $4.99 a month or $39.99 a year for 300 replies a month, and a $4.99 one-time purchase removes the banner. Your own key stays free and unlimited. Testers move to the store version when it ships; nothing is lost.

Does it work without internet?

The camera side does: USB-C and the camera's own Wi-Fi Direct need no internet at all. The coach does need a connection, because the frame goes to the AI provider either way.

What about Canon and Nikon?

Coming, no date yet. Canon uses its own PTP extensions, so it is real work rather than a switch to flip; Nikon follows Canon.

Can I try it without a camera?

Yes. The app opens on a built-in demo camera, marked with a green DEMO badge, and everything except the physical link works exactly as it does with real hardware, coach included.
