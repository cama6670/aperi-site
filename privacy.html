Privacy Policy: Aperi

Aperi ("the app", "we") helps you control a tethered camera and get AI photography coaching. This policy explains what the app collects, where data goes, and your choices. Plain-language summary first; specifics follow.

Your photographs are the sensitive thing here, so this policy is unusually specific about where they go.

The short version

  • The app has no accounts and no analytics or tracking SDKs of our own.
  • Your API keys and conversation history stay on your device.
  • When you ask the coach about a shot, that photo + camera settings + your message are sent to an AI provider to generate a reply. That is the one time your photograph leaves the device. With your own key it goes straight to the provider you chose (Google Gemini, Anthropic Claude, or OpenAI) and never to us. With the Aperi coach, the default, it passes through our relay on the way to OpenAI; the relay forwards it and keeps none of it. The app asks for your consent before the first send either way.
  • Which provider you choose, and which tier, decides what happens to your photographs. See the section below: this genuinely differs between providers, and a free tier is not the same as a paid one.
  • If ads are shown, the Google Mobile Ads (AdMob) SDK collects advertising identifiers. Removing ads (one-time purchase) stops ad requests.

Read your AI provider's terms. This part is not ours to control.

When you bring your own API key, the request goes from your phone straight to that provider, under your own account. We are not in the path, we never see the image, and we cannot change what that provider does with it. Their terms govern, not ours.

That distinction matters more than it might sound, because the providers differ:

  • Google Gemini, free tier. Google states that content submitted on the unpaid tier is used to provide, improve and develop Google's products, and that human reviewers may read it. For this app, that content is your photographs. If that is not acceptable to you, do not use a free-tier Gemini key here.
  • Google Gemini, paid tier. Google states that paid-tier prompts and responses are not used to train or improve its models.
  • OpenAI API. OpenAI states that API inputs and outputs are not used to train its models by default, and are retained for a limited period (published as up to 30 days at the time of writing) for abuse monitoring, then deleted.
  • Anthropic API. Anthropic states that API inputs and outputs are not used to train its models by default.

Verify these against each provider's current terms before you send anything you would mind being read. They are summarised here in good faith and they change; the provider's own policy is the authority, not this page. Links are in the app next to each provider's key field.

Our key will never be used to share your photographs for AI training

The Aperi coach runs on our API key instead of yours, so the coach works the moment you open the app. It is the default; you can switch to your own key in Settings at any time. On the Aperi coach we commit that:

  • We will not enable any data-sharing or model-training option on the accounts we operate. Our provider's API default is that API data is not used for training, and we will not opt in to anything that changes it.
  • We will not sell, share, or license your photographs, to anyone, for any purpose.
  • We will not use your photographs to train models of our own.
  • Frames are relayed and dropped. Our metering records counts, costs and outcomes, never image data, never your prompts, never the coach's replies.
  • The provider may still retain data briefly for its own abuse monitoring, as described above. That is outside our control and we will not claim otherwise.

What the relay keeps. Not your photograph, not your question, not the reply. Those are passed to the provider and dropped. What is written down is the accounting needed to run a metered service:

  • a per-user identifier (see below), the time of the request, which AI model answered it, how many tokens it used, what it cost us, how long it took, whether it succeeded, and the size in bytes of the image
  • usage rows are deleted after 90 days
  • day-by-day totals are kept longer, with no identifier attached. They say how many people used the coach, never who
  • a suspension record, if we ever have to block an account for abuse, is kept for as long as the block stands
  • all of it is stored in the European Union, wherever you are. Your photograph still travels to the AI provider in the United States, because that is where the model runs, under the safeguards their data processing terms provide.

How we know it is you, without an account. There are no usernames and no passwords, and we would rather not start now.

  • If you subscribe on iPhone, we use the transaction identifier Apple signs for your purchase. It identifies the subscription, not you: no name, no email. On Android, the app sends your Google Play purchase token to our relay, which verifies it with Google's Play Developer API; the relay stores only a hash of the token, never the token itself.
  • On the free tier on iPhone, we use App Attest, an Apple feature that confirms the request came from a genuine, unmodified copy of this app. It gives us a meaningless-looking key that lets us count your daily prompts. Deleting and reinstalling the app clears it and gives you a fresh one. We considered a mechanism that survives reinstalling and deliberately rejected it, because it would have meant setting an identifier you could not get rid of.
  • On the free tier on Android, we use Play Integrity, Google's equivalent. The app sends an integrity token from Google with every free-tier request; to mint it, Google receives the app's package name and the device's integrity state, under Google's privacy policy. The app also generates a random install identifier, kept in encrypted storage on the device and sent to us only once, when it registers. It identifies an install, not a person, and uninstalling the app discards it.

Erasing it. A button in the app deletes everything above for you. You will not need to email us or quote an identifier. The one exception is a suspension record, which we keep so that erasing your data cannot lift a ban. If that ever applies to you, the app will say so rather than claiming everything went.

If you use your own key instead, none of this applies to you: with your own key we operate nothing in the path between your phone and the provider.

What the app stores on your device

  • API keys: in the platform's app-private store (Android DataStore / iOS Keychain). Never transmitted anywhere except, as a bearer credential, to the matching AI provider's own API endpoint over HTTPS. Never logged, never sent to us.
  • Coaching history: your questions, the live-view frames you sent, the camera settings at that moment, and the coach's replies, saved locally so you can revisit them. You can clear this at any time in the app; uninstalling removes it.
  • Preferences: chosen AI provider/model, connection mode, camera IP, toggles. Local only.
  • Camera Wi-Fi credentials: if you connect over Wi-Fi, the camera's access-authentication password is stored on the device and used only to open an encrypted connection to the camera on your local network. It is never sent to us.
  • The last shot: the most recent photograph the camera handed the app is kept on the device so the coach can be asked about it after a relaunch, and, on iPhone, so the Apple Watch app can show it.

What is sent off the device, and to whom

  1. AI coaching requests, with your own key → the provider you select in Settings:
    • Google Gemini API (generativelanguage.googleapis.com)
    • Anthropic Claude API (api.anthropic.com)
    • OpenAI API (api.openai.com)

    Each request contains the live-view image or the last shot, structured camera settings, your typed or spoken text, and recent conversation context. What each provider then does with it is covered in the section above.

  2. AI coaching requests, with the Aperi coach → our relay (proxy.aperi.photo, run on Cloudflare) and on to the OpenAI API. The relay forwards the same content and keeps the accounting described in the section above, in the European Union. It never stores the image, your text, or the reply.
  3. Purchases → on iPhone, RevenueCat, Inc., which validates your receipt with Apple and tells the app what you are entitled to. RevenueCat receives purchase events and an anonymous identifier it generates. It is never given a photograph, a prompt, or an API key. See RevenueCat's own privacy policy. On Android, Google Play Billing, and, for a coach subscription, the purchase token to our relay as described above. Payment itself is handled by Apple or Google; we never see your card details.
  4. Voice input → the platform's speech recognizer, when you dictate a question: Apple on iPhone and Apple Watch, Google on Android. Either may send the audio to the platform's servers for transcription, or transcribe it on the device, depending on the device and its settings. The audio is your question, never your photograph. Type instead of speaking if you would rather it stayed on the device.
  5. Ads → Google AdMob, which may collect an advertising ID and device or coarse location data to serve and measure ads, per Google's policies. Buying "Remove ads" stops all ad requests and that use of the advertising ID. A consent prompt is shown where required (for example EEA and UK).
  6. Apple Watch (iPhone only) → your own watch. The last shot, your question and the coach's reply travel between the phone and the paired watch over Apple's device-to-device link, not through us.

With your own key, we operate nothing that receives your photographs or messages. With the Aperi coach, the relay described above is the one thing of ours in the path, and what it keeps is listed there.

Microphone, camera connection, network

  • Microphone is used only while you hold or activate voice input, to transcribe your spoken question. The app does not record the audio or save it anywhere. The platform's speech recognition may process that audio on Apple's or Google's servers rather than on the device, under their privacy policies, which is how dictation works generally. The audio is your question, not your photograph: images are never part of it.
  • Hands-free coaching, when you turn it on, keeps the app running with the screen off so an earbud press can reach it: a background audio session on iPhone, a foreground service on Android. The microphone is used in that mode only for the question you dictate after a press, as above.
  • USB / Wi-Fi access is used solely to communicate with your camera. Over Wi-Fi the app opens an encrypted connection to the camera on your local network; nothing about that connection is sent to us.
  • Internet access is used for AI requests, purchases, and ads.

Children

Not directed to children under 13 (16 in some regions). No knowing collection of children's data.

Your choices

  • Choose your own key or the Aperi coach, and change your mind at any time. The coach never sends a photograph until you ask it to, and asks for your consent before the first send to each destination.
  • Choose a paid provider tier if you do not want your photographs used to improve that provider's products.
  • Clear coaching history in-app. If you use the Aperi coach, the same screen erases what our relay recorded, for both your free-tier and subscription identities.
  • Remove ads via one-time purchase to stop ad data collection.
  • Uninstalling deletes all local app data.
